# KrishiSetu — Business Model & Impact

## 1. Where the money comes from (production model)

| Revenue stream | Mechanism | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| **Platform commission** | 3–5% of order value on completed sales (vs 25–45% total intermediary margin today) | Farmer/FPO |
| **Logistics fee** | ₹29 retail / weight-slabs for bulk; free above threshold to drive volume | Buyer |
| **FPO SaaS subscription** | ₹500–2,000/month for aggregation tools, crop-planning analytics, bulk-quote desk | FPOs |
| **Bulk contracts** | Match fee on fulfilled quote contracts (hotels, messes, retailers) | Bulk buyer |
| **Value-added services** | Working-capital tie-ups, crop insurance, packaging, cold chain (phase 2) | Partners/farmers |

**Why farmers still win:** a farmer selling tomatoes at ₹26/kg on KrishiSetu keeps ~₹24.7
(after 5%) versus ~₹18 from the agent chain — a **~35% uplift** — while the consumer pays
~₹26–28 instead of ₹32+. The platform earns by *removing* margin, not adding one.

## 2. Unit economics (demo-scale illustration)

| Metric | Value (from seeded demo data) |
|---|---|
| Avg consumer saving vs mandi-retail | **~19–20%** (live KPI on landing) |
| Avg farmer realisation uplift vs mandi farm-gate | **~20%** (live KPI) |
| Delivery cost per stop | ~₹18–25 (optimised routes, 8–10 stops/round) |
| Route distance saved by optimizer | **~15–25%** per round → direct margin |

## 3. Impact model

**For farmers / FPOs**
- Higher, transparent, same-day-priced realisation; no distress sales (AI price floor signal)
- Demand forecasts → plan what to sow/harvest; bulk quotes → guaranteed offtake
- FPOs get digital aggregation infrastructure (the 10,000-FPO government push)

**For consumers**
- Fresher produce (harvest-date shown), traceable to a named farm
- 15–25% cheaper than mandi-linked retail

**For the system (DoCA's lens)**
- Fewer touchpoints → less post-harvest loss (India loses ~₹90k cr/yr in produce)
- Price transparency complements consumer-affairs mandates
- Route optimization → fewer vehicle-km → lower emissions

## 4. Go-to-market

1. **Supply first** — onboard FPOs around one metro (Pune pilot in the demo), guarantee
   buy-up via bulk contracts with 3 anchor buyers (messes/hotels).
2. **Demand density** — society-level "farm baskets", subscribe-and-save weekly orders.
3. **Logistics piggyback** — aggregate existing shivan/tempo return trips before owning fleet.
4. **Scale** — replicate hub-playbook city by city; add cold chain where produce mix demands it.

## 5. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Supply volatility | Forecast-driven planting plans + multi-FPO pooling per crop |
| Quality disputes | Hub-side grading (photo AI later), grade A/B badges, ratings |
| Payment trust | Escrow payout on delivery confirmation (demo: simulated UPI) |
| Farmer digital literacy | Marathi/Hindi voice-first app, FPO-assisted onboarding |
| Cold-chain cost | Slot-based density routing (already in optimizer), perishable-only hubs |
